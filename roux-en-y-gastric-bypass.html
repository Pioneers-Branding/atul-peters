Procedure Overview

What is Roux-en-Y Gastric Bypass?

First open gastric bypass was described by Mason and Ito in 1966. It has been modified into its current form using a Roux-en-Y limb of intestine (RYGB). The RYGB is one of the most commonly performed operations for weight loss all over the world, with the first laparoscopic gastric bypass performed by Wittgrove and Clark in 1994.

Principle of Roux-en-Y Gastric Bypass

Roux-en-Y Gastric bypass surgery is an operation in which a small pouch of stomach is created and joined to the small intestine after bypassing an initial segment of small intestine known as duodenum and jejunum, thereby diverting bile as well as intestinal secretions.

Aim of this arrangement: To achieve long term weight loss with least side effects and complications, leading to resolution of obesity and its related comorbidities.

Mechanism of Weight Loss: The net effect of this re-arrangement is a restricted intake of food; and due to the bypass, some malabsorption also takes place. The intestine secretes hormones (ghrelin, GIP, GLP, PYY) when exposed to undigested food, modifying metabolism so that diabetes and blood pressure are resolved or controlled. There is reduction in hunger as well as early satiety after small meals.

Expected Outcomes

Weight Loss: Studies done at Center of Excellence in bariatric surgery all over the world report about 80% of excess weight loss after roux-en-y gastric bypass over a period of 18 months.

Resolution of other problems: The obesity-related comorbidities that may be improved or cured with the RYGB include:

  • Type 2 diabetes mellitus & High blood pressure
  • High cholesterol & Heartburn
  • Arthritis & Obstructive sleep apnea
  • Urinary stress incontinence & Liver disease
Surgical Aspects

Laparoscopic & Robotic Gastric Bypass

Roux-en-Y Gastric Bypass (RYGB) Surgery is done laparoscopically, or by Robotic Assistance and is still the “Gold Standard” in the world. All other procedures are usually compared to this when it comes to the results in terms of excess weight loss and resolution of co-morbidities.

Pre-Op & Positioning

Every patient is evaluated by a team of experts before being taken for surgery, which includes body composition analysis, dietary consults, and multidisciplinary medical evaluation.

  • Mandatory thromboembolism prophylaxis 6-12 hours before surgery
  • Placed in reverse Trendelenberg’s position under anesthesia
  • 3 to 4 small operating incisions (ports) are used
  • Closed by special glue allowing the patient to bathe quickly

Gastric Pouch & Bypass

The surgery can be performed laparoscopically or with Robotic Assistance, which provides high-definition magnified optics and extreme precision for suturing.

  • Formation of a small gastric pouch using surgical staplers
  • The pouch is formed on a special gastric calibration tube
  • About 100 to 170 cm of the small intestine is bypassed
  • Intestines re-attached by precise stapling or suturing mechanisms

Dietary Guidance

Post-Surgery Diet: Phase I

The first step in your recovery is a structured clear liquid phase. Here are the essential Do's and Don'ts.

✓ Do's

  • Non-aerated, clear liquids
  • Sip slowly throughout the day (50 to 100ml every hour)
  • Aim for 2 to 2.5L per day
  • Stop immediately if you feel full, pain or discomfort

✗ Don'ts

  • Avoid sweetened beverages (calories + dumping syndrome risk)
  • No carbonated beverages (can stretch the pouch)
  • No alcoholic drinks (absorbed faster, higher intoxication)
  • Never use straws (introduces air, causes discomfort)

Frequently Asked Questions

9 of the most common questions about Roux-en-Y Gastric Bypass, answered by Dr. Atul Peters. NCR.

Can an abdominal or groin hernia heal without surgery if I wear a hernia belt?

No. A hernia is a mechanical physical hole or structural tear in your muscular fascia. Hernia belts, trusses, or abdominal binders merely apply external compression to temporarily keep the bulge pushed inside while you are wearing them; they do not close or repair the torn muscle layer. Over time, constant pressure from a tight belt can cause skin ulceration and make the underlying muscle edges more scarred and difficult to repair.

Will I experience weight gain or weight loss after my gallbladder is removed?

Gallbladder removal surgery itself does NOT cause weight gain or weight loss. Your metabolism and caloric burning rate remain entirely unchanged. However, many patients who were previously skipping meals or eating poorly out of fear of gallstone pain suddenly find they can enjoy food comfortably after surgery. Maintaining a balanced diet and regular exercise ensures healthy weight management.

What happens if I have asymptomatic (silent) gallstones discovered on a routine checkup?

If your gallstones are truly asymptomatic and under 1 cm, immediate surgery is not mandatory for everyone. However, prophylactic (preventive) laparoscopic cholecystectomy is strongly recommended if: (1) you have large stones exceeding 2 to 3 cm, (2) you have small multiple stones under 3 mm (high risk of slipping), (3) you have diabetes, or (4) your gallbladder wall is thickened or calcified (Porcelain Gallbladder).

How long do the keyhole incisions take to heal, and will I have visible scars?

Our 4K laparoscopic and robotic techniques utilize tiny incisions measuring just 5mm to 10mm. We close these incisions using internal absorbable sutures and surgical tissue glue, eliminating the need for outer skin stitches. Within 10 to 14 days, the skin closes completely. Over several months, these tiny marks fade into virtually imperceptible hairline creases that are barely visible.

Can gallstones form again after my gallbladder is surgically removed?

Once your gallbladder is completely removed by laparoscopic cholecystectomy, gallstones cannot form in the gallbladder again because the organ itself is gone. In extremely rare instances (less than 0.5% of cases), a small stone may form inside the bile ducts (primary CBD stones) due to slow bile transit, which is easily managed via endoscopy (ERCP) without surgery.
