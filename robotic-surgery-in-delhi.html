What is Robotic-Assisted Surgery?

Robotic surgery is the most advanced form of minimally invasive (keyhole) surgery available today. It is important to note that the robot does not perform the surgery autonomously; instead, it is directly controlled by an expert surgeon.

Unmatched Precision

Robotic surgery is one of the most advanced and precise forms of minimally invasive surgery available today. In this technique, the surgeon performs the operation using a specialized robotic system, which allows for exceptional accuracy, enhanced vision, and better control. Unlike open surgery that requires large incisions, robotic surgery involves making tiny cuts through which surgical instruments and a high-definition camera are inserted.

Robotic surgery has transformed the way complex operations are performed, especially in areas that are difficult to access with traditional techniques. In procedures involving the abdomen, pelvis, or chest — such as bariatric surgery, gallbladder surgery, hernia repairs, and anti-reflux surgery — robotic assistance allows the surgeon to work with unmatched precision and care.

How Da Vinci Robotic Surgery Works

Understanding the three core components of the Da Vinci Surgical System.

1. The Surgeon Console

This is where Dr. Peters sits during the operation. He views a high-definition 3D image of the surgical site that is magnified up to 10 times. His hand, wrist, and finger movements at the console are translated in real-time to the robotic instruments.

2. The Patient Cart

Positioned near the patient, the cart holds the camera and the surgical instruments. These highly advanced robotic arms mimic the surgeon's movements but with greater range of motion, allowing for extreme dexterity in tight spaces.

3. The Vision Cart

This cart makes communication between the components possible and supports the 3D high-definition vision system. It also provides a widescreen view of the surgery for the rest of the assisting operating room team.

Step-by-Step

Procedure of Robotic Surgery

The robotic surgery process typically follows these steps. Throughout the procedure, the surgeon is in complete control. The robot does not act independently; it amplifies the surgeon's capabilities.

1. Preparation

The patient is put under general anesthesia.

2. Insertion of Instruments

Small incisions are made, and robotic arms are inserted.

3. Console Operation

The surgeon controls the robotic arms via a console, guiding tools and camera precisely.

4. Surgical Task

The procedure is performed using enhanced visualization and steady robotic hands.

5. Closure & Recovery

Incisions are closed, and the patient is moved to recovery.

Post-Op Experience

What to Expect During Recovery

Robotic surgery dramatically reduces the physical toll of surgery, allowing for a much faster return to your daily life.

0-24 Hours (Hospital)

Most patients are out of bed and walking within a few hours of surgery. Due to the minimal incisions, pain is well-controlled. Depending on the complexity of the procedure (like bariatric or complex hernia), discharge often occurs within 24 to 48 hours.

Days 1-7 (Home)

You will likely experience only mild soreness. Patients are encouraged to take light walks to promote circulation. Most people can comfortably return to desk work, driving, and normal daily routines within a week.

Weeks 2-6 (Full Activity)

The tiny 8mm incisions heal rapidly, leaving virtually invisible scars. By week 4 to 6, patients can typically resume all strenuous activities, including heavy lifting and intense exercise, without restriction.

Aftercare

Post-Care after Robotic Surgery

Recovery after robotic surgery is generally smoother and quicker than traditional open surgery. Proper post-operative care is crucial for optimal healing.

Pain Management & Wound Care

Pain is usually mild and well-controlled with oral medications. Incisions are closed with dissolvable sutures or skin adhesive. Keep the area clean and dry.

Mobility and Activity

Light movement such as walking is encouraged within a few hours after surgery to improve blood circulation and reduce the risk of blood clots.

Dietary Instructions

Depending on the procedure, dietary modifications may be advised. For bariatric or abdominal surgeries, a soft or liquid diet might be recommended initially.

Follow-Up Appointments

Regular follow-up visits are scheduled to monitor healing and review test results. Dr. Peters’ team also provides emotional and lifestyle counseling.
