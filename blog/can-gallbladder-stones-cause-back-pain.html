Can Gallbladder Stones Cause Back Pain? Symptoms & Solutions

Why Gallstone Pain Radiates to the Back

When people think of gallstones, they usually imagine pain localized strictly to the upper right side of the abdomen. However, experienced gastrointestinal surgeons frequently evaluate patients whose primary complaint is intense, aching pain in the upper back or between the shoulder blades.

Because back pain is so commonly attributed to muscle strain, poor sitting posture, or spinal disc issues, many patients spend weeks taking painkillers or visiting physiotherapists without relief—unaware that the true culprit is a gallbladder filled with gallstones! The Science of "Referred Pain" in Gallbladder Disease

The gallbladder is a small pear-shaped organ located directly beneath the liver in the upper right abdomen. Its primary job is to store and concentrate bile, a digestive fluid produced by the liver to break down fats.

When you consume a meal containing fats, the gallbladder contracts to squeeze bile through the narrow cystic duct into the small intestine. If a gallstone lodges in the neck of the gallbladder or blocks the cystic duct, the organ squeezes forcefully against the obstruction.

This intense muscular spasm causes rapid pressure buildup and organ distension. The visceral nerve fibers supplying the gallbladder (the celiac plexus and phrenic nerve) enter the spinal cord at the exact same neurological segments (T7–T9) that receive sensory nerve signals from the lower right shoulder blade and upper back. Similar referred pain diagnostic patterns occur in upper stomach conditions, detailed in our post on hiatus hernia ultrasound detection.

The brain misinterprets these shared spinal cord signals, perceiving the visceral gallbladder pain as severe pain coming from the upper back muscle tissue—a phenomenon known medically as referred pain (or Boas' sign).

How to Differentiate Gallstone Back Pain from Muscle Strain

Meal Connection Flares 30–90 min after fatty meals No relation to food intake
Posture Relief Lying, stretching, sitting gives NO relief Rest & posture adjustment relieves pain
Associated Symptoms Nausea, vomiting, abdominal bloating Local muscle tenderness on pressing

Associated Warning Symptoms to Watch For

Gallstone back pain rarely occurs in isolation. It is typically accompanied by digestive red flags. For other acute GI risks, see our article on how to prevent appendicitis:

  • Biliary Colic: Severe pain in the right upper quadrant lasting from 30 minutes to several hours.
  • Nausea & Indigestion: Feeling uncomfortably full or nauseous after modest meals.
  • Jaundice (Yellowing of Eyes/Skin): Occurs if a gallstone slips into the main bile duct (choledocholithiasis).
  • Fever & Chills: Indicates acute infection of the gallbladder (cholecystitis), requiring emergency hospitalization.

Long-Term Digestive Health After Gallbladder Surgery

Following laparoscopic gallbladder removal, your body adapts quickly to direct bile secretion from the liver into the duodenum. For the first few weeks, adopting a light, low-fat diet helps prevent temporary digestive discomfort. Over time, patients resume enjoying normal meals without fear of gallbladder pain attacks.

Definitive Treatment for Symptomatic Gallstones

Once gallstones become symptomatic (causing back or abdominal pain attacks), medications or gallstone-dissolving syrups cannot permanently cure them. Leaving gallstones untreated risks serious complications such as acute cholecystitis (gallbladder infection), jaundice, or gallstone pancreatitis.

The gold-standard treatment is Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy. Through 3 to 4 tiny keyhole incisions, the surgeon removes the diseased gallbladder along with the stones. After surgery, the liver drips bile directly into the small intestine, allowing normal digestion without pain.
