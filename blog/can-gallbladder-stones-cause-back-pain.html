When people think of gallstone symptoms, they usually picture severe pain in the upper right abdomen. However, many patients visiting Dr. Atul Peters report a different symptom: intense upper back pain or an ache between the shoulder blades.

Because back pain is so commonly associated with muscle strain, posture issues, or spinal discs, patients are often surprised when an ultrasound reveals that their back pain was actually caused by gallstones!

Why Do Gallstones Cause Back Pain? (The Science of Referred Pain)

The gallbladder is located under the liver in the upper right quadrant of the abdomen. When a gallstone temporarily blocks the cystic duct, the gallbladder squeezes forcefully against the blockage, creating intense pressure.

The nerve pathways (specifically the celiac plexus and phrenic nerve) that sense pain in the gallbladder share spinal cord nerve roots with the upper back and right shoulder area. The brain interprets these shared nerve signals as pain in the back—a phenomenon called referred pain.

How to Distinguish Gallstone Back Pain from Muscle Strain

  • Timing: Gallstone pain often begins 30 to 90 minutes after eating a meal containing fats or oils.
  • Character: Constant, deep, aching, or squeezing pain that lasts from 30 minutes to several hours.
  • Movement: Unlike muscle strain, changing positions, stretching, or resting does NOT relieve gallstone pain.
  • Associated Symptoms: Nausea, vomiting, bloating, and upper abdominal tightness.

Definitive Treatment for Symptomatic Gallstones

Once gallstones begin causing back or abdominal pain, medications or gallstone-dissolving pills are ineffective. The gold-standard treatment is Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy—a quick 30-minute keyhole procedure to remove the gallbladder, allowing patients to go home the next day.
