Can Hiatus Hernia Be Visualised on Ultrasound? Diagnostic Accuracy Guide

If you suffer from severe acid reflux or chest discomfort, can an ultrasound scan confirm a hiatus hernia? Here is how doctors accurately diagnose hiatus hernias and when laparoscopic repair is needed.

Key Takeaways

  • Ultrasound has significant limitations: Standard abdominal ultrasound is generally NOT reliable for diagnosing a hiatus hernia because the hiatus lies behind the ribcage and sternum.
  • Gold-Standard Diagnostic Tests: Upper GI Endoscopy (EGD) and Barium Swallow X-Ray are the primary diagnostic tools.
  • High-Resolution Esophageal Manometry: Measures esophageal sphincter pressures and hiatal muscle integrity.
  • Effective Surgical Treatment: Minimally invasive Laparoscopic Nissen Fundoplication permanently repairs the hiatal opening.

A hiatus hernia occurs when the upper part of the stomach pushes upward through the diaphragm muscle into the chest cavity. Because the esophageal sphincter muscle becomes displaced, stomach acid easily flows upward into the esophagus, causing severe GERD (gastroesophageal reflux disease), heartburn, and chest pain.

Patients frequently undergo an abdominal ultrasound for digestive symptoms and wonder why their scan report did not mention a hiatus hernia. In this clinical breakdown, Dr. Atul Peters explains why ultrasound has limitations and which specialized diagnostic tools provide definitive answers.

Can Ultrasound Detect a Hiatus Hernia?

In most clinical settings, standard abdominal ultrasound cannot reliably visualize a hiatus hernia. Here is why:

  • Anatomical Position: The esophageal hiatus is located deep in the lower mediastinum behind the lower ribcage and sternum, where ultrasound waves cannot penetrate clearly.
  • Interference from Intestinal Gas: Air and gas inside the stomach obscure ultrasound visualization of the gastroesophageal junction.
  • Dynamic Movement: Sliding hiatus hernias move up and down depending on body posture, which static ultrasound probes often miss.

What Tests Accurately Diagnose a Hiatus Hernia?

To confirm the size, type, and severity of a hiatus hernia, gastrointestinal specialists utilize:

1. Upper GI Endoscopy (EGD)

A thin, flexible camera inserted through the mouth allows direct visual assessment of the esophagus, stomach lining, and hiatal ring tightness.

2. Barium Swallow X-Ray

The patient swallows a liquid contrast solution while real-time X-rays capture the stomach sliding into the chest cavity during swallowing.

3. High-Resolution Esophageal Manometry

Measures the precise muscle contraction strength of the lower esophageal sphincter (LES) to guide surgical planning.

When Is Surgery Recommended for Hiatus Hernia?

If medication (PPIs) fails to control acid reflux, or if a large paraesophageal hernia poses risk of stomach twisting, keyhole laparoscopic hernia repair in Delhi combined with fundoplication restores normal anatomy with 24-hour hospital stay.
