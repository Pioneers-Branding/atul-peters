What Is a Laparoscopic Hysterectomy (TLH)?

A hysterectomy—the surgical removal of the uterus—is one of the most frequently performed gynecological operations worldwide. Historically, removing the uterus required a large 6-to-8-inch open abdominal incision (similar to a C-section), resulting in significant post-operative pain, blood loss, long hospital stays, and a 6-week recovery period.

Thanks to modern minimal access surgical breakthroughs, women frequently ask: "Can my uterus be removed using keyhole laparoscopic surgery without a large abdominal cut?" The answer is an unequivocal YES.

In a Total Laparoscopic Hysterectomy (TLH), the entire uterus and cervix are detached from internal pelvic ligaments and blood vessels using ultra-precise laparoscopic instruments inserted through 3 or 4 tiny 5mm keyhole incisions. The uterine tissue is then safely removed, and the upper vagina is closed laparoscopically with microscopic precision. Similar minimal access innovations apply to upper GI conditions like evaluating whether a hiatus hernia is visualised on ultrasound.

Medical Indications for Uterus Removal

A laparoscopic hysterectomy is recommended for non-cancerous and chronic conditions unresponsive to medical management:

  • Multiple or Large Uterine Fibroids (Myomas): Benign muscular tumors causing heavy menstrual bleeding, pelvic pain, and bladder pressure.
  • Severe Endometriosis & Adenomyosis: Ingrowth of uterine lining tissue into pelvic organs or muscular uterine walls, causing debilitating period pain.
  • Abnormal Uterine Bleeding (AUB): Prolonged, heavy menstrual bleeding leading to severe chronic anemia.
  • Pelvic Organ Prolapse: Sagging of the uterus into the vaginal canal due to weakened pelvic ligaments.
  • Early Gynecological Premalignancies: Pre-cancerous changes in the endometrial lining.

Key Benefits of Laparoscopic Hysterectomy vs. Open Surgery

  1. Minimal Post-Op Pain: Because major abdominal muscles are not cut, pain is dramatically lower, requiring minimal pain medication.
  2. Tiny Cosmetic Scars: Keyhole marks measure only 5mm to 10mm and fade to near-invisible lines over time.
  3. Low Blood Loss & Infection Risk: High-definition camera magnification allows meticulous sealing of uterine blood vessels.
  4. Short Hospital Stay: Patients walk comfortably the evening of surgery and are discharged within 24 to 48 hours.
  5. Rapid Return to Normal Life: Women return to light desk work and normal routines within 10 to 14 days, compared to 6 weeks for open surgery.

Detailed Step-by-Step Surgical Workflow for TLH

During a Total Laparoscopic Hysterectomy, specialized 4K camera optics magnify pelvic nerve structures and ureters ten times, allowing the surgical team to seal uterine arteries with minimal thermal damage. Just as in gallbladder keyhole surgery for gallbladder stone pain, keyhole precision preserves surrounding tissue.

  • Abdominal Access: Gentle insufflation of CO2 gas creates a clear, safe operating space in the pelvis.
  • Vessel Sealing: Bipolar vessel sealing devices close uterine and ovarian vessels without suture slipping.
  • Vaginal Vault Closure: The vaginal cuff is sutured using advanced barbed continuous sutures, ensuring robust pelvic support.

Long-Term Health & Quality of Life After Laparoscopic Hysterectomy

Women undergoing Total Laparoscopic Hysterectomy report significant improvements in energy, mood, and quality of life once severe anemia, heavy period bleeding, and pelvic pressure are eliminated. If ovaries are preserved, natural estrogen production continues uninterrupted, supporting bone density and cardiovascular health.
