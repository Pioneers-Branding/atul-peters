Can Uterus Be Removed by Laparoscopy? (Laparoscopic Hysterectomy Guide)

Wondering if a hysterectomy can be done without a large abdominal incision? Learn how Total Laparoscopic Hysterectomy (TLH) allows minimal pain, 24-hr discharge, and rapid recovery.

Key Takeaways

  • Yes, absolutely: Over 90% of uterine removals today can be safely performed using laparoscopic keyhole surgery.
  • Total Laparoscopic Hysterectomy (TLH): Performed using 3 to 4 tiny 5mm keyhole incisions with high-definition camera guidance.
  • Faster Recovery: 24 to 48 hours hospital stay compared to 5–7 days for open abdominal surgery.
  • Da Vinci Robotic Precision: For complex uterine fibroids or severe pelvic adhesions, robotic surgery in Delhi provides unmatched dexterity.

A hysterectomy—the surgical removal of the uterus—is one of the most common surgical procedures performed for women worldwide. In the past, hysterectomy required a large 6-to-8-inch open incision across the lower abdomen, involving significant pain and a 6-week recovery.

Thanks to advancements in minimal access technique, women frequently ask: "Can my uterus be removed by laparoscopy?" The answer is a clear YES. Total Laparoscopic Hysterectomy (TLH) has revolutionized gynecological surgery.

Common Indications for Laparoscopic Uterus Removal

A laparoscopic hysterectomy is recommended for benign and chronic conditions including:

  • Large Uterine Fibroids: Non-cancerous growths causing heavy bleeding and pelvic pressure.
  • Severe Endometriosis or Adenomyosis: Causes debilitating menstrual pain and chronic pelvic discomfort.
  • Abnormal Uterine Bleeding (AUB): Heavy bleeding unresponsive to medical management.
  • Pelvic Organ Prolapse: Descending of the uterus due to weakened pelvic floor ligaments.

Key Advantages of Laparoscopic Hysterectomy

  1. Tiny Invisible Scars: Performed through small 5mm to 10mm keyhole marks.
  2. Minimal Blood Loss & Lower Infection Risk: High-definition magnified optics allow precise blood vessel sealing.
  3. Short Hospital Stay: Patients walk the same evening and are discharged within 24 to 48 hours.
  4. Return to Work in 2 Weeks: Normal light activity resumes far faster than traditional open surgery.
