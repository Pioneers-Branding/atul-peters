Procedure Overview

What is Sleeve Gastrectomy?

Laparoscopic/Robotic sleeve gastrectomy which is also known as vertical sleeve gastrectomy is a procedure in which about 70 to 80% of the stomach is removed leaving a narrow gastric tube or "sleeve" through which food passes. It is free from serious anatomical intestinal complications in the long run like internal herniation or small bowel obstruction, and the micronutrient deficiencies and malnutrition are less frequent.

Mechanism of Weight Loss

Sleeve Gastrectomy is no longer considered purely a restrictive surgery but also a metabolic surgery.

1. Restrictive Mechanism: Due to this small stomach tube, the amount of food ingested is restricted and a person can take small amount of food at a time, thereby, the amount of calories consumed is restricted which results in weight loss.

2. Hormonal Mechanism: The removed part of the stomach includes fundus, which is the upper outer part of stomach. It is important as it releases hormone Ghrelin. This hormone is termed as ‘hunger hormone’ because it stimulates appetite, increases food intake and promotes fat storage. By removal of fundus, the source of Ghrelin is removed.

Clinical Outcomes

  • Weight Loss: About 80% of the excess weight in a select subgroup of patients.
  • Hospital Stay: It is a simple procedure and the patients may be able to leave the hospital in one day or even the same day in selected cases.
  • Stomach Removed: About 70 to 80% of the stomach is removed.
  • Intestinal Bypass: No intestines are removed or bypassed so that malabsorption is minimal.

Dietary Guidance

Life After Sleeve Surgery: Do's & Don'ts

After Sleeve reconstruction, most patients can resume their diet fairly quickly. There are generally no specific long-term dietary restrictions imposed by the surgery itself.

Initial Recovery & Liquids

After Sleeve reconstruction, most patients can resume their diet fairly quickly. Once the patient is comfortable and able to tolerate fluids, they are usually started with small sips of water, followed by a full-liquid diet for about a day.

Advancing to Normal Diet

The diet is then gradually advanced to soft foods and, in most cases, a normal diet within the next 2–3 days, depending on individual tolerance and recovery.

Things to Avoid

There are generally no specific long-term dietary restrictions imposed by the surgery itself. However, patients are encouraged to avoid alcohol, smoking, and highly processed or junk foods during the recovery period.

Protein-Rich, Balanced Diet

Adequate protein provides the building blocks required for tissue repair and healing, supports muscle strength, and helps the body recover after surgery. Patients should therefore include good sources of protein such as milk and dairy products, eggs, pulses, legumes, soy, fish, chicken, or other suitable protein-rich foods, as tolerated.

Protein Supplements

If dietary intake is insufficient or meeting protein requirements through regular meals is difficult, a protein supplement may also be added to help bridge dietary gaps and support adequate protein intake during recovery.

Hydration & Individualized Advice

Staying adequately hydrated and choosing nutrient-dense foods can further support healing and a smoother return to normal activities. Dietary advice may be individualised based on the patient’s medical condition, dietary preferences, tolerance, and the type of surgery performed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about undergoing safe, permanent Sleeve reconstruction with Dr. Atul Peters in Delhi NCR.

Can an abdominal or groin Sleeve heal without surgery if I wear a Sleeve belt?

No. A Sleeve is a mechanical physical hole or structural tear in your muscular fascia. Sleeve belts, trusses, or abdominal binders merely apply external compression to temporarily keep the bulge pushed inside while you are wearing them; they do not close or repair the torn muscle layer. Over time, constant pressure from a tight belt can cause skin ulceration and make the underlying muscle edges more scarred and difficult to repair.

Will I experience weight gain or weight loss after my Sleeve is removed?

Sleeve removal surgery itself does NOT cause weight gain or weight loss. Your metabolism and caloric burning rate remain entirely unchanged. However, many patients who were previously skipping meals or eating poorly out of fear of gallstone pain suddenly find they can enjoy food comfortably after surgery. Maintaining a balanced diet and regular exercise ensures healthy weight management.

What happens if I have asymptomatic (silent) gallstones discovered on a routine checkup?

If your gallstones are truly asymptomatic and under 1 cm, immediate surgery is not mandatory for everyone. However, prophylactic (preventive) laparoscopic cholecystectomy is strongly recommended if: (1) you have large stones exceeding 2 to 3 cm, (2) you have small multiple stones under 3 mm (high risk of slipping), (3) you have diabetes, or (4) your Sleeve wall is thickened or calcified (Porcelain Sleeve).

How long do the keyhole incisions take to heal, and will I have visible scars?

Our 4K laparoscopic and robotic techniques utilize tiny incisions measuring just 5mm to 10mm. We close these incisions using internal absorbable sutures and surgical tissue glue, eliminating the need for outer skin stitches. Within 10 to 14 days, the skin closes completely. Over several months, these tiny marks fade into virtually imperceptible hairline creases that are barely visible.

Can gallstones form again after my Sleeve is surgically removed?

Once your Sleeve is completely removed by laparoscopic cholecystectomy, gallstones cannot form in the Sleeve again because the organ itself is gone. In extremely rare instances (less than 0.5% of cases), a small stone may form inside the bile ducts (primary CBD stones) due to slow bile transit, which is easily managed via endoscopy (ERCP) without surgery.
